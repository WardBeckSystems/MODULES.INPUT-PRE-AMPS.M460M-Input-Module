> CHAPTER 3
>
> The M460LA Input Module (14")
>
> The M460 series of input modules had numerous subscripts running from M460 to M460M. Because of the application of M460 modules to almost 100% custom consoles, if WBS were able to use an exsisting M460 design, they did. If not, they knocked off a new M460 and gave it the next available subscript letter. M460LA is a coincidence. Another was the M460C - used for consoles provided to CBS-TV.
>
> As the company grew...
>
> Ron and Roger eyes were always on every project. Obviously every customer did not require a custom design, particularly as the company grew and "standard" console designs were implemented - M1203, M1204, L2042, L3242, R1000, R1200, R2000, and T1202. WBS had a Systems Engineering Dept., which grew to about 10 people, to implement small to moderate customization using existing modular elements. The company reached 110 people at it's peak, in two buildings, at 841 Progress Ave., Scarborough.

— **Theanswerman**, Dec 20, 2010|43

> CHAPTER 15
>
> I don't think WBS design people took much from other manufacturers. Rather WBS output was most affected by customer requests and demands. Virtually every early console was a custom design, using exsisting modules where they exsisted, and producing new modules when required. Take the M460 input module as an example - M460, M460A, M460B...thru to... M460M.
>
> As customer budget restraints entered the picture more and more, WBS made a valiant effort to offer "standard" or "stock" designs at considerable savings. The M1203 (table top) and M1204 (full frame) were early stocks using the most sophisticated M460 type modules. The T1202 (table top) developed from the 55 CBC custom small consoles (the banana jacks were a CBC requirement) provided for the 1976 Montreal Olympics. The stock T1202 (more than 100 were made) used new M480 series modules (banana jacks replaced by XLR). For larger stock consoles the L2042 and L3242 units were introduced, now with 470 series modules. The M472 EQ series were about half the cost of the M462 EQ series, because the M472 EQ was not "stand alone", did not have balanced isolated IN and OUT circuitry, but instead caused the equilization to occur interstage within the M470 series input module. Finally there were standard R1200 and R2000 Radio Consoles. The standardization was the frame wiring, and then MIC, LINE, or STEREO input modules could be bought and inserted in any configuration.

## M460M from Dan Alexander

_WBS INFO · Apr 5, 2010|43_

> Hello WBS fans.
>
> A little bit of info for you. A fellow by the name of Dan Alexander had acquired one or two older WBS consoles with M460M modules. This module comes with conductive plastic pots on the EQ and was the deluxe EQ for the M460 series (a four band was designed but never pressedin to production). The console(s) that Dan has were of a series that were for a few TV stations and record labels (one owned the other at the time I believe) down in the USA. As you know most of the WBS modules are actively balanced. This is a good thing and we will leave it at that. HOWEVER these customers wanted transformers on the output. So WBS being a custom shoppe, made appropriate transformers. NOW if you buy these modules, clean them well and enjoy them - I believe his price is reasonable. However you certainly do NOT need the transformers to put after the modules in your console nor your rack to make these modules from Dan work properly. The clients wanted the direct outs of the modules to be either 600 ohms or 150 ohms - depending upon their application. The boards were manufactured with either a 600 ohm transformer or a 150 ohm transformer - not a 2 in 1 sort of thing. Now, the fine people at Dan's shoppe do not know the windings of the transformers, and are busy thus un-able to measure them. But I wanted to make you aware that if you decide to purchase these lovely modules you do NOT need the transformers at all. And for the record I purchased two units from him, and use them with my M460A modules regularly. None of which have the output transformer. But if you do feel that you want one for isolation or something - feel free to use it, and enjoy it but be advised to have it measured to see if its impedance will match your system's requirements.

— **warrenbeck**, Apr 5, 2010|43

## M460LA Manual

_WBS INFO · Nov 12, 2011|44_

> Tony has a couple of manuals here for the M460 series. I use them to maintain all my M460 modules. THey are all essentially the same except for additional routing switches, and terminology - Fold Back, PA, Reverb Send, etc... the evil module is the M - of which I have 4. They were made for 1 customer and there were 2 large boards supplied. The boards made it to Dan Alexander this year and he parted them out. They were engineered not by Dad but the engineering department of building 1. They are a nightmare, very different internal wiring from the rest of the M460 series and Dad was not amused with that evolution, so much so that he waded back in and designed and directed every project form that point on except for the Renaissance consoles - he had absolutely nothing to do with those with the exception of suggesting the control room volume pot be an infinite rotation optical type pot. The M460M is a 3 band fully parametric EQ with an additional daughter amplifyer board jury-rigged in place to provide a line level feed to a FOH board. The boards were initially used for game shows, then 1 went off to do sound track work and the FOH mod was used to go to a smaller board for headphone mixes and producers feeds. 
> The stupidity of the M460M (OK I am rambling and wayyyy off topic) is the wiring of the Pendar switch (CH ON thing) it was not done to standard convention, which was done to save locating (1) Shadow switch to the extrusion, and floating it off the mother PCB (like the CUE on an M490 module)!!! 
>
> Regards,

## M460C module questions

_WBS RACKMOUNTING · Jul 16, 2012|45_

> All M460 series modules are pretty much the same for current draw, and out-put. The only odd duck was the M460M. It was grudgingly designed by Engineering in building 1 and they made a huge mess of it. 
>
> Please go to the manuals section that Tony has assembled for us and make reference to any M460 module there. Either use the direct out or the fader out. You will have TONNES of headroom and gain. Tom successfully sells racked M460's to many, many happy clients world wide, and I use M460's in desk-top towers in my studio almost daily.

## M461M schematic

_WBS TECHNICAL SUPPORT · Feb 15, 2013|46_

> The M461 is standard across all M461 modules - regardless of the suffix. The suffix typically denotes the option of a customer special routing. Largely they are all interchangeable, hot swappable, etc... just have to wire your console for the auxes/summing/RS/FB/PA etc...
>
> The ONLY exception to this is the M460M - it was designed after some whyning by the engineering department, and not by Dad. They did silly things to the switch, and routing. It can all be re-configured to be a normal M460 (straight through to and including LA for pin-outs not necessarily function). But after that headache all modules conformed to 1 consistent specification for its series.
